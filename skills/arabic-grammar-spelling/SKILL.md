Use this for Arabic grammar, spelling, punctuation and correcting Arabic text.
1. Middle hamza follows the stronger vowel of the hamza and the letter before it (kasra, then damma, then fatha): ئ in سُئِل and بِئر, ؤ in مُؤمن and يُؤدي, أ in سأل and رأس. After a long alif or a silent waw it often sits on the line (قراءة، تساءل، مروءة).
2. Initial hamza: أ with fatha or damma, إ with kasra. Hamzat al-wasl (written without hamza) in ال، ابن، اسم، امرأة، اثنان، the trilateral imperative (اكتب) and five- and six-letter verbs and their verbal nouns (انطلاق، استخدام).
3. Final hamza follows the vowel before it: ئ after kasra (شاطئ), ؤ after damma (تباطؤ), أ after fatha (بدأ), on the line after a silent letter or long vowel (شيء، سماء).
4. Taa: ة ends feminine nouns and adjectives (مدرسة، جميلة) and is read as h at a pause; ت appears in verbs and sound feminine plurals (كتبت، معلمات) and in nouns such as بيت.
5. Final alif: ى when the root's last letter is ya (مشى، هدى) and in words of four letters or more (مستشفى), except after ya (دنيا، يحيا); ا when it comes from waw in three-letter words (دعا، سما).
6. Numbers: 3 to 10 take the opposite gender of the singular counted noun (ثلاثة كتب، ثلاث سيارات); 11 to 99 take a singular accusative noun (خمسة عشر كتابًا); 100 and 1000 take a singular genitive (مئة كتاب).
7. Punctuation: Arabic comma (،), semicolon (؛) and question mark (؟), no space before a mark.
8. When correcting, keep the author's meaning and style, list the main corrections with the rule, and leave dialect words unless asked.

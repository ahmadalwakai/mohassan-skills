Use this for writing, analysing or explaining Arabic poetry.
1. Clarify form and purpose: a classical vertical poem (عمودي) with one meter and one rhyme, free verse (تفعيلة), or Nabati and colloquial poetry; the theme, the addressee, the number of lines, and the tone (praise, love, elegy, wisdom, pride, longing).
2. Classical meters and their feet, for example:
   - الطويل: فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن in each half.
   - البسيط: مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فعلن.
   - الكامل: متفاعلن متفاعلن متفاعلن.
   - الوافر: مفاعلتن مفاعلتن فعولن.
   - الرجز: مستفعلن مستفعلن مستفعلن.
   - الرمل: فاعلاتن فاعلاتن فاعلن.
   - المتقارب: فعولن فعولن فعولن فعولن.
   - الخفيف: فاعلاتن مستفع لن فاعلاتن.
3. Scansion (التقطيع): write the line as pronounced (spoken letters count, silent ones drop, a doubled letter counts twice, tanween counts as a nun), mark moving and silent letters, group them into feet, and name permitted variations (زحافات وعلل) instead of calling every change an error.
4. Rhyme: the same rawi letter and vowel pattern throughout, and no early repetition of the same rhyme word (الإيطاء).
5. Quality: fresh images over clichés, meaning that progresses, correct grammar and case endings in MSA, no filler words added only for the meter.
6. Honesty about meter: when unsure that every line scans, say so and show the scansion of one line rather than claiming the whole poem is correct.
7. Attribution: famous verses are quoted with the poet's name, never presented as new.
Answer shape: the poem with clear line breaks (two halves per line in classical forms), then the meter name and one scanned line when useful.

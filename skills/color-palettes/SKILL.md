Use this for choosing or reviewing colors for a brand, site or app.
1. Begin with meaning and audience: the feeling the brand should give (trust, energy, calm, luxury, freshness), the conventions of the sector, and what competitors use, so the palette fits in or stands apart on purpose.
2. Structure: one primary brand color, one accent for calls to action, a neutral range (near-black text, greys, off-white backgrounds), and semantic colors (green success, amber warning, red error, blue info). Roughly 60 percent neutral, 30 percent secondary, 10 percent accent.
3. Build tints and shades of each color (steps 50 to 900) by changing lightness in HSL or OKLCH, so states and backgrounds stay in the family.
4. Contrast (WCAG): normal text at least 4.5:1 against its background; large text (24 px, or 19 px bold) and interface parts at least 3:1. Check every pair; saturated brand colors often fail as small text on white.
5. Color is never the only signal; pair it with text or icons.
6. Dark mode is its own palette: dark grey rather than pure black, slightly desaturated colors, contrast checked again.
7. Culture: meanings vary by region; green carries positive and religious associations across much of the Arab world; test choices with the real audience.
8. Sector habits: food and hospitality lean on warm reds, oranges and earth tones; finance and health on blues and greens; confirm the choice still differs from the main competitors.
Answer shape: a palette table (role, name, HEX, usage), contrast results for the key pairs, and a short rationale.

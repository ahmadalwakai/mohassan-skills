Use this when someone is choosing between products, services or options to buy.
1. Needs before specs: main uses, must-haves, nice-to-haves, budget range, country (prices, warranty and availability differ), and what they use now.
2. Compare only what matters for those needs, in a table: price, key specs translated into real effect (battery hours, low-light camera, weight), build and reliability, years of software updates, warranty and service network, running costs, resale value.
3. Use current, checkable information: specifications and prices change and new models replace old ones. State the date of the information and recommend checking the live price and stock; remembered prices are never presented as current.
4. Reviews: independent expert reviews and patterns across many user reviews outweigh single opinions and sponsored content; look for recurring faults.
5. Laptops for programming: at least 16 GB of RAM, a fast SSD of 512 GB or more, a good keyboard and screen, and the operating system the work needs; integrated graphics are enough unless the work involves games, 3D or machine learning.
6. Phones: years of update support, camera needs, battery, size and ecosystem (iPhone with other Apple devices; Android for flexibility and choice).
7. Cars and other large purchases: total cost of ownership (fuel or charging, insurance, maintenance, depreciation), reliability records, and a test drive or hands-on trial.
8. Give a clear recommendation for the stated needs, and say when the cheaper option is good enough.
Answer shape: a short comparison table, the recommendation with its reason, and what would change it.

Use this to write persuasive marketing copy: ads, product descriptions, sales pages and posts.
1. Know the reader and the single action: who they are, what they want, what worries them, what they already know, and the one thing the copy must make them do.
2. Lead with the benefit in their words, then support it: what they get (outcome), why believe it (numbers, reviews, guarantees, credentials), why now (a real reason: season, limited slots, a new offer), and what to do next (a specific call to action).
3. Frameworks: AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action) for ads and emails; PAS (problem, agitation, solution) for pain-driven services; features → advantages → benefits for product descriptions.
4. Headlines: specific beats clever ("Same-day tyre fitting at your home in Glasgow" beats "Tyres made easy"); use numbers, the customer's problem or the outcome, and write several to test.
5. Product descriptions: who it is for and the main benefit first, then sensory and practical details, sizes and specs as a scannable list, and answers to the usual objections (fit, care, delivery, returns).
6. Voice: plain, active and confident; short sentences; more "you" than "we"; no superlatives without proof and no claim that cannot be backed up.
7. Arabic copy is written natively rather than translated, in the dialect or MSA that suits the audience, with short punchy lines.
8. Respect ad platform rules and consumer law: no false scarcity, fake reviews, misleading prices or health claims.
9. Offer two or three versions with different angles (benefit, proof, emotion) for testing.
Answer shape: the copy ready to use, then two alternative angles, with character counts when the platform has limits.

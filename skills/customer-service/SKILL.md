Use this for complaints, angry customers, reviews and service standards.
1. Reply fast and personally: acknowledge within hours, use the customer's name, and show you understood the specific problem in your own words.
2. A good reply: acknowledge → apologize for the experience without blaming the customer → explain briefly what happened, without excuses → say exactly what you will do and by when → offer a fair remedy (refund, redo, discount, priority) → give a direct contact → follow up after the fix.
3. Angry customers: stay calm, let them finish, do not argue or lead with policy, agree on the facts, then move to solutions; offer a call when writing escalates.
4. Delivery delays: the real reason in one sentence, the new time, what is being done to secure it, and a gesture proportional to the inconvenience.
5. Public reviews (Google, Trustpilot): answer every negative review within a day or two, politely and briefly, with no private details, and invite the customer to continue privately; thank positive reviewers too. Fake reviews and rewards for positive ones only break platform rules and, in many countries, consumer law.
6. Refunds follow the published policy and the law; when bending the policy, say it is a one-time exception.
7. Learn from complaints: log category, cause and fix, review monthly, and fix root causes in the process.
8. Standards: response-time targets, a tone guide, reply templates that staff personalize, and authority for frontline staff to settle small issues on the spot.
Answer shape: a ready reply (with an alternative tone if useful), then one or two process fixes to prevent a repeat.

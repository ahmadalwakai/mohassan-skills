Use this for CVs, cover letters, LinkedIn profiles and job applications.
1. Tailor to the target job: take the job ad, pick its key requirements and keywords, and make sure the CV shows evidence for each one.
2. Structure, newest first: name and contact (phone, email, city, LinkedIn); a three-line profile aimed at the role; experience (title, employer, place, dates, then 3 to 6 bullets); education; skills; certifications; languages. One page early in a career, two at most later.
3. Bullets show results, not duties: action verb + what was done + measurable outcome ("Cut invoice processing time 40% by automating approvals in Excel"). Estimate honestly when exact numbers are unknown, and never invent.
4. ATS-friendly format: standard headings, a single-column layout, no tables, text boxes or images for key content, common fonts, PDF or DOCX as requested, keywords used naturally.
5. Regional norms: in the UK and US no photo, age or marital status; in the Gulf a photo, nationality and visa status are common; follow the employer's expectations.
6. Cover letter of about 250 to 400 words: why this role and company, two or three matching achievements, what the candidate brings, a confident close, addressed to a named person when possible.
7. LinkedIn: a headline with role and value, an About section in the first person, featured achievements, skills that match target roles.
8. Interviews: STAR answers (situation, task, action, result) for common questions, research on the company, and good questions to ask.
Answer shape: the complete CV or letter ready to paste, [placeholders] for unknown details, and the three most important changes made.

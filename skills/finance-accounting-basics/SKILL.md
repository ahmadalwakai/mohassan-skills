Use this for small business finance and accounting questions. Figures for filing need an accountant's check.
1. Profit versus cash: profit is revenue minus expenses for a period; cash flow is money actually in and out. A profitable business still runs out of cash when customers pay late, stock is bought ahead or loans are repaid.
2. Income statement: revenue − cost of goods sold = gross profit (gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue); − operating expenses (rent, salaries, marketing, fuel, insurance) = operating profit; − interest and tax = net profit.
3. Monthly cash flow forecast: opening cash + receipts − payments = closing cash, including VAT payments, loan repayments, owner drawings and seasonal swings. Watch the lowest point.
4. Balance sheet: assets (cash, receivables, equipment, stock) = liabilities (payables, loans, tax owed) + equity.
5. Break-even: fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit) = units needed. Example: fixed costs 6,000 a month, price 100, variable cost 40 → 100 jobs a month.
6. Margin and markup differ: a 50% markup on cost is a 33% margin on price.
7. Working capital: invoice promptly with clear terms, chase late payments, take deposits, negotiate supplier terms, keep stock lean.
8. Tax differs by country: VAT rules and rates (20% standard rate in the UK with a registration threshold; 15% in Saudi Arabia), corporate or income tax, payroll. Keep receipts, separate business and personal accounts, and use accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, Zoho Books).
9. Never present tax figures as final; flag what an accountant should confirm.
Answer shape: the calculation step by step with a small table, then what it means for decisions.

Use this for hiring, onboarding and managing people.
1. Define the role before advertising: purpose, outcomes expected in the first 6 to 12 months, must-have versus nice-to-have skills, salary range, location and hours.
2. Job advert: a title people actually search for, a short pitch on why the job is worth taking, responsibilities as outcomes, a few real requirements, salary and benefits, how to apply, inclusive wording. No discriminatory criteria (age, gender, nationality) unless legally justified.
3. Screening: a few knock-out questions, then a short phone call.
4. Structured interviews: the same core questions for every candidate, scored against set criteria. Behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time you handled an angry customer") and practical work samples, for example a short paid trial. For developers: a realistic small task or pairing session, plus questions on debugging, trade-offs and past projects.
5. Check references and right to work before the offer; put salary, start date and probation terms in writing.
6. Onboarding: a first-week plan, tools and access ready on day one, a buddy, clear 30-60-90 day goals.
7. Performance: regular one-to-ones, specific feedback (situation, behaviour, impact), measurable goals, recognition, documented improvement plans when needed.
8. Retention comes from fair pay, clarity, autonomy, growth and a manager who listens.
9. Employment law differs by country (contracts, hours, leave, dismissal); recommend local rules or an HR professional for disputes.
Answer shape: the requested document (advert, interview guide, plan) ready to use, with scoring criteria where relevant.

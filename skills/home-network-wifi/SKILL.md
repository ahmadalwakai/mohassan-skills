Use this for home Wi-Fi, internet speed and router problems.
1. Locate the problem: run a speed test next to the router, then in the weak room, and over a cable if possible. Slow everywhere, even on a cable, points to the line or the provider; slow only far away points to Wi-Fi coverage.
2. Quick fixes: restart the modem and router (power off for 30 seconds), update the router firmware, and move the router to a central, raised, open spot away from microwaves, thick walls, mirrors and metal, never inside a cabinet.
3. Bands: 2.4 GHz reaches further but is slower and crowded; 5 GHz (and 6 GHz on Wi-Fi 6E and 7) is faster over shorter range. Use 5 GHz for nearby devices and pick a quieter channel or automatic selection.
4. Coverage across rooms: a mesh system (several units acting as one network) is the best fix for larger homes; a wired access point or a powerline adapter with Wi-Fi comes next; simple repeaters often halve speed.
5. DNS: a fast public resolver (for example 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8, or 9.9.9.9 with malware filtering) can make browsing feel quicker but does not raise download speed. Set it on the router for every device, or per device in network settings.
6. Security: change the router's default admin login, use WPA3 (or WPA2-AES) with a long Wi-Fi passphrase, turn off WPS, keep firmware updated, and put guests and smart-home devices on a guest network.
7. Heavy use: look for background downloads and updates, enable QoS on the router if it has it, and upgrade the plan only after the Wi-Fi itself is fixed.
8. Line faults (drops at the same time every day, slow even on a cable) go to the provider with the test results.
Answer shape: one or two diagnostic questions if needed, then steps from easiest to most effective with the improvement to expect from each.

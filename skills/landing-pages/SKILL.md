Use this to plan, write or review a landing page or a small business website.
1. One page, one goal: decide the single conversion (call, booking, quote request, purchase, sign-up), the audience and the traffic source (ads, search, social); the message must match what brought the visitor.
2. Above the fold: a headline stating the outcome for the customer in plain words (what you do, for whom, where), a supporting line with the main proof or difference, one primary call to action with a specific label ("Get a free quote"), and a relevant real image. Local services show the phone number.
3. Section order that suits most services: problem or desire → solution and how it works in three steps → benefits rather than features → proof (named reviews, ratings, client logos, numbers, before and after) → pricing or what affects price → FAQ that answers objections (cost, time, insurance, guarantees) → final call to action.
4. Trust: real photos of team and work, licences and insurance, address and service area, guarantees, payment security marks, recent reviews.
5. Form: as few fields as possible (name, phone or email, one or two qualifying questions), inline validation, a thank-you page that says what happens next.
6. Mobile first: main content visible in under about 2.5 seconds, a sticky call or booking button, tap-to-call, text readable without zooming.
7. Measure conversions (form submits, call clicks, calls), then A/B test one element at a time: headline, offer, button label, form length.
8. Every claim must be true and provable; never invent reviews, prices or awards.
Answer shape: a section-by-section outline with draft copy, then design notes and the conversion events to track.

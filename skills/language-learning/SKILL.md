Use this to plan or coach learning a language.
1. Set the goal and level: the reason (work, study, travel, an exam), the current CEFR level (A1 to C2), time per day and the deadline. Reaching a comfortable B1 from zero usually takes several hundred hours, so set honest expectations.
2. A daily routine of 30 to 60 minutes beats occasional long sessions: about a third input (listening and reading that is mostly understandable), a third active practice (speaking, writing), a third vocabulary and review.
3. Vocabulary: the most frequent words first (the top 1000 to 2000 cover most daily speech), learned in phrases and sentences rather than lists, reviewed with spaced repetition (Anki or a similar app), plus words met in real use.
4. Listening and speaking: shadowing (repeating audio right after the speaker), content at slow then natural speed, learner podcasts, and conversation with a tutor or exchange partner from the first weeks; record yourself to check pronunciation.
5. Grammar: learn patterns in context, then short targeted explanations for repeated mistakes; grammar study without use does not stick.
6. Writing: short daily texts (a journal, messages) corrected by a teacher, a partner or a careful tool, then rewritten.
7. Measure progress every four to six weeks: a short placement test and a recorded speaking sample compared with earlier ones.
8. Arabic speakers learning English: early focus on vowel sounds, the p/b and v/f contrasts, articles, the verb "to be" and word order. Learners of Arabic: script and sounds first, then MSA or a dialect chosen by goal.
Answer shape: a weekly plan with daily tasks, resources by type, and milestones.

Use this to find, organize and synthesize studies on a topic.
1. Turn the topic into a focused question; for health and social topics use population, intervention or exposure, comparison and outcome.
2. Search strategy: list key concepts, synonyms and the English equivalents of Arabic terms; combine them with AND, OR and quotation marks; filter by date and study type. Use Google Scholar, PubMed (health), ERIC (education), IEEE Xplore or ACM (computing), Scopus or Web of Science where available, and university or national digital libraries for access.
3. Weigh evidence: systematic reviews and meta-analyses, then randomized trials, then cohort and case-control studies, then cross-sectional and qualitative work, with expert opinion last. Note sample size, setting and funding.
4. Screen in two passes, titles and abstracts against inclusion criteria and then full texts, recording why studies were excluded.
5. Extract into a table: author and year, country, design, sample, measures, main findings, limitations.
6. Synthesize by theme rather than study by study: where findings agree, where they conflict and why (methods, populations), and what is missing (the research gap).
7. Cite accurately and never invent a study, author, year or result. A reference that cannot be verified is marked as unverified.
8. State the date of the search so readers know how current the review is.
Answer shape: search terms and databases, a summary table of key verified studies, then the thematic synthesis and the gap.

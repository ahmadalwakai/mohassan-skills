Use this for creating or reviewing a logo and a visual identity.
1. Brief first: business name, what it does, audience, market (local or international; Arabic, English or both), three adjectives for the desired feel, competitors' logos, and where the logo will live (sign, van livery, app icon, social avatar, invoices).
2. Choose the logo type for the name and its uses: wordmark (distinctive name), lettermark or monogram (long names), symbol with wordmark (most small businesses), emblem (heritage, badges).
3. Develop several concepts from the brand idea rather than sector clichés (a moving company does not need a truck: consider care, the box, the arrow, home, motion). Sketch in black and white first; a strong mark works in one color.
4. Test: legible at 16 px and on a vehicle from a distance; works in one color, reversed on dark and over photos; distinct from competitors; no accidental shapes or meanings in either language.
5. Bilingual logos: draw Arabic and Latin lettering as a matched pair (similar weight, height and character), decide the lockups (side by side, stacked) and which language leads in each market.
6. Deliverables: vectors (SVG, PDF, EPS) and PNG at common sizes; full color, one color, reversed, horizontal, stacked and icon-only versions; clear-space and minimum-size rules.
7. Identity system: primary and secondary colors with HEX, RGB and CMYK values, typefaces, photo and icon style, tone of voice and sample applications (card, letterhead, social post, signage) in a short guidelines document.
8. Never copy or closely imitate an existing trademark; run a basic trademark search before adopting the final design.
Answer shape: two or three concepts (idea, form, colors, fonts), the recommended one, and the files and rules to produce.

Use this to solve math problems step by step and to make sensible estimates.
1. Restate what is asked, list the known values with units, and name the unknown.
2. Choose the method, then solve in clear steps, one operation per line, keeping units throughout.
3. Order of operations: brackets, powers, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Percentages: x% of y = y × x ÷ 100; percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100; a 20% rise followed by a 20% fall does not return to the start.
4. Equations: apply the same operation to both sides to isolate the unknown (2x + 5 = 17 → 2x = 12 → x = 6), then substitute back to check.
5. Practical geometry: rectangle area = length × width (a 4 m × 5 m room is 20 m²); for tiles, divide the area by one tile's area and add about 10% for cuts and breakage; convert units before calculating.
6. Probability: favourable outcomes ÷ equally likely outcomes; multiply for independent events; for "at least one" use 1 − P(none).
7. Estimation (Fermi): split the quantity into factors you can estimate, round to easy numbers, multiply, and give the order of magnitude with the assumptions stated.
8. Verify: check the answer against the question, test it with a rough estimate or by substitution, and confirm the units and size make sense.
9. When a calculator tool is available, use it for exact arithmetic.
Answer shape: the steps in order, the final answer clearly marked with units, and a one-line check.

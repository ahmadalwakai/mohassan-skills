Use this for structuring, writing and designing presentations.
1. Purpose and audience first: what the audience should know, feel and do at the end, the length of the slot, and whether the deck is presented live or read alone (read-alone decks need more text).
2. Structure as a story: situation → problem → solution → evidence → plan → ask. State the main message in the first minute and repeat it at the end.
3. Investor pitch in 10 to 15 slides: problem, solution, why now, market size built bottom-up, product, business model, traction, competition and advantage, go-to-market, team, financials and key metrics, the ask and the use of funds.
4. One idea per slide; the title is the takeaway sentence ("Repeat customers doubled in six months"), not a topic label. Plan roughly one slide per one to two minutes of speaking.
5. Text: few words on screen (often under 30), at least 24 pt when presented, no paragraphs; speaker notes carry the detail.
6. Visuals: one strong chart or image per point, the key number highlighted, simplified charts (no heavy gridlines, direct labels), consistent fonts, colors and alignment from a master template.
7. Honest data: sources on charts, bar charts starting at zero, no cherry-picked periods.
8. Delivery: rehearse aloud with a timer, prepare answers to the three hardest questions, keep a PDF backup, check the screen ratio (16:9 by default).
9. Arabic decks: a right-to-left template, an Arabic font that renders correctly in the presentation software, consistent digits.
Answer shape: a slide-by-slide outline (title sentence, content, visual), then design notes.

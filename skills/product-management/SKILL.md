Use this for product decisions: what to build, in what order, and why.
1. Start from problems, not features: who the users are, the job they are trying to get done, how they do it today, and the evidence that the problem matters (interviews, support tickets, usage data, lost deals).
2. Define outcomes: the metric a feature should move (activation, retention, conversion, time saved) and the target.
3. Prioritize transparently: RICE (reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort), value versus effort, or MoSCoW for a fixed release; score with the team, then apply judgement on strategy fit and dependencies. Most ideas get a no.
4. An MVP is the smallest version that tests the riskiest assumption with real users, not a weaker version of everything.
5. PRD in one to three pages: problem and evidence, goals and non-goals, users and use cases, requirements as user stories with acceptance criteria ("As a customer I can reschedule a booking up to 24 hours before, so that..."), design links, metrics, risks and open questions, rollout plan.
6. Roadmap by themes and outcomes (now, next, later); dates only for real commitments.
7. Ship and learn: release behind feature flags or to a small group, measure against the target, talk to users, then iterate or remove.
8. Keep stakeholders aligned with a short written rationale for each major decision.
Answer shape: the prioritized list with scores and reasoning, or the PRD in headings, plus the metric that will show success.

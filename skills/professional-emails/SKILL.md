Use this for professional emails, letters and work messages.
1. Know the purpose and the one action wanted from the reader, and put it in the first two lines.
2. Subject line: specific and scannable ("Leave request: 3 to 7 March", "Invoice 1042 overdue since 10 May").
3. Structure: greeting with the right title and name → purpose in one sentence → needed context (facts, dates, numbers, references) → the clear request or next step with a deadline → polite close and full signature (name, role, phone).
4. Tone by relationship: formal for senior people, authorities and new clients; warm but concise for colleagues; never sarcastic. Arabic official letters use formal MSA openings (السيد/السيدة، تحية طيبة وبعد); internal notes can be lighter.
5. Apologies: name the specific issue, take responsibility without excuses, state the fix and what changes to prevent a repeat, offer a fair remedy, keep it short.
6. Payment reminders escalate gradually: a friendly reminder with the invoice attached → a firm reminder with amount, due date and late terms → a final notice stating the next step. Always include the invoice number, amount and payment details.
7. Requests to a manager (leave, raise, change): dates or specifics, a cover plan, flexibility, and thanks.
8. Keep it short: three short paragraphs or bullets, one email per topic.
9. Never invent facts the sender did not provide; mark gaps as placeholders such as [date].
Answer shape: subject line, then the ready-to-send message; for important letters, one formal and one friendlier version.

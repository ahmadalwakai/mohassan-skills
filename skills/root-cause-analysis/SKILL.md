Use this to find why a problem happens, especially when it keeps coming back.
1. Describe the problem as a measurable fact: what, where, since when, how often, how big ("on-time deliveries fell from 95% to 78% since 1 March, mostly in afternoon slots").
2. Check the data first: is the change real (tracking, definitions, seasonality, holidays), and where does it concentrate (product, region, channel, staff, time)? Comparing where it happens with where it does not narrows the causes quickly.
3. Five whys: ask why repeatedly, each answer backed by evidence, until reaching a cause that, once fixed, stops the recurrence. Stop at process or system causes, not at a person to blame.
4. Fishbone (Ishikawa) to brainstorm causes by category: people, process, equipment, materials, environment, measurement; for services add policies and suppliers.
5. Test the top candidate causes with data or a small experiment before acting.
6. Sudden drop in sales: check tracking and the website first (broken forms, payment, outages), then traffic by source (paused ads, ranking, budget), then conversion (price change, stock, competitor offers, reviews), then seasonality and market events.
7. Fix at the root with a process change, not only a reminder, and add a check or alert that catches it early next time.
8. Verify with the same measure after the fix, and write a short blameless post-mortem: what happened, impact, cause, fix, prevention.
Answer shape: the most likely root cause with its evidence chain, other causes to rule out, and the fix with how to verify it.

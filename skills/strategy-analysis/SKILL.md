Use this for strategic analysis and choosing where and how to compete.
1. Define the scope and the question: the business or unit, the market, the time horizon and the decision at stake (grow, enter, defend, reposition, exit).
2. External view: PESTEL for the political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal forces that matter in the next few years; Porter's five forces for industry attractiveness (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power).
3. Internal view: resources and capabilities, costs, brand, customer base, data and people; what is valuable, rare and hard to copy.
4. SWOT done well uses specific, evidenced items ("4.8 rating from 600 reviews", not "good service"), then turns into actions with TOWS: strengths to capture opportunities, fixes for weaknesses that block opportunities, strengths against threats, and retreat where weakness meets threat.
5. Competing with bigger rivals: pick a focus they serve poorly (a niche, an area, a speed or premium promise), compete on service, flexibility and relationships rather than price, build local reputation and reviews, and partner where scale matters.
6. Generic positions: cost leadership, differentiation or focus. A business with no clear reason to be chosen is the common failure.
7. Options: list at least three realistic strategies, judge each on attractiveness, feasibility, risk and fit, and choose with explicit trade-offs (what you will not do).
8. Make it executable: three to five priorities with measures, owners and a first 90-day step.
Answer shape: the analysis in a compact table or framework, then the recommended strategy, its trade-offs and first steps.

Use this to summarize texts, documents, recordings or meetings.
1. Match the summary to its use: a one-line gist, a short paragraph, key points or a structured brief, at any requested length. Default to about 10 to 20 percent of the original for articles.
2. Read for structure first: the main claim or purpose, the supporting points, the evidence and the conclusion.
3. Lead with the main point, then supporting points in order of importance rather than order of appearance.
4. Keep facts exact: names, numbers, dates and quotes as in the source. Never add facts, opinions or conclusions that are not there, and keep the source's own uncertainty visible.
5. Separate what the author claims from what is established, with attribution ("the report says").
6. Drop repetition, examples and anecdotes unless they are the point.
7. Meeting minutes: date, attendees and purpose; decisions (one line each); action items as a table of task, owner and deadline; open questions; next meeting. Record decisions and actions, not a transcript.
8. Several or long documents: summarize each part, then synthesize across them, noting agreements and contradictions.
9. If the source text was not provided or could not be read, say so instead of summarizing from memory.
Answer shape: a one-sentence takeaway, then bullets (or the minutes template), with the source named.

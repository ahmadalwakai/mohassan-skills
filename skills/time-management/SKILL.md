Use this for planning days and weeks, studying, focus and productivity.
1. Capture everything first: tasks, deadlines, fixed commitments (work hours, classes, prayer times, family) and energy patterns (when the person focuses best).
2. Prioritize with the Eisenhower matrix (urgent and important: now; important, not urgent: scheduled; urgent, not important: delegated or batched; neither: dropped) and at most three key outcomes per day.
3. Time-block the calendar: fixed commitments first, deep work in the best energy hours in 60 to 90 minute blocks, shallow tasks batched, about 20% buffer for the unexpected, breaks and sleep protected.
4. Study plans: spaced repetition and active recall (self-testing) beat rereading; 25 to 50 minute sessions with short breaks (Pomodoro); mixed subjects; plan backwards from exam dates with review rounds.
5. Procrastination: make the first step tiny and specific ("open the file and write the heading"), remove friction and distractions (phone in another room, site blockers), use implementation intentions ("at 9:00 at my desk I will..."), and reward progress.
6. A 30-minute weekly review: what got done, what slipped and why, next week's priorities, a calendar check.
7. Habits: attach new habits to existing routines, start small, track streaks, plan for missed days without quitting.
8. Rest, family and health belong in the plan; an overloaded schedule fails.
Answer shape: a schedule table (time, activity) or a weekly plan, plus two or three techniques suited to the person's situation.

Use this to plan trips: itineraries, budgets, transport, accommodation and documents.
1. Basics first: origin, destinations, dates or flexibility, number and ages of travellers, budget level, interests, pace, and needs such as halal food, accessibility, prayer times or children.
2. Documents: passport validity (often at least six months), visas or electronic travel authorizations (such as the UK ETA or the EU's ETIAS where they apply), travel insurance. Requirements depend on nationality, so the traveller confirms them on the official government site.
3. Transport: compare door-to-door time and total cost, not only the ticket price. Trains: book early for advance fares, travel off-peak, check railcards and passes. Flights: compare baggage fees and airport transfer costs. Between European cities, trains often beat flights for journeys under about four hours.
4. Itinerary: group sights by area to cut travel, one or two main activities a day plus free time, opening days and advance booking for popular sights, realistic travel times between places.
5. Accommodation: location first (near transport or the main areas), recent reviews, cancellation terms, the total price with taxes and fees.
6. Budget table: transport, accommodation, food, activities, local transport, SIM or roaming, insurance, and a 10 to 15% buffer.
7. Prices, timetables and availability change: figures are estimates to be checked live with the operator before booking; remembered fares are never presented as current.
8. Practicalities: local emergency numbers, payment methods, plug type, weather and dress norms, copies of documents saved.
Answer shape: a day-by-day itinerary, a budget table and a short booking checklist.

Use this to understand users and remove friction from a product flow.
1. Define the goal and the metric: which task should get easier (sign-up, checkout, booking) and how success is measured (completion rate, time on task, errors, drop-off at each step).
2. Find where people fail: funnel analytics show where they leave, session recordings and heatmaps show how, support tickets and reviews show why.
3. Usability test: five participants who match the real audience reveal most major problems in a flow. Give realistic tasks without naming interface elements ("book a cleaning for next Friday"), ask them to think aloud, stay neutral, and note hesitation, errors and give-ups. Rate issues by frequency and impact.
4. Interviews: ask about past behaviour, not wishes ("tell me about the last time you..."), avoid leading questions, listen for workarounds and for the words people use.
5. Heuristic review (Nielsen): visible system status, real-world language, user control and undo, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, efficiency for experts, minimalist design, clear error recovery, help when needed.
6. Fixes that often reduce drop-off: fewer fields (only what is needed now), guest checkout, passkey or social sign-in, progress indicators, prices and fees shown early, autofill support, saved progress, trust signals next to the decision.
7. Personas and journey maps come from real data; label assumptions as assumptions.
8. Validate changes with an A/B test or a before-and-after metric, not opinion.
Answer shape: the likely problems ranked by impact, the evidence for each, and the change to test.
